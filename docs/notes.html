Bench notes / Audio

LA4440 stereo amplifier with volume, mute and a digital-pot upgrade path

2 x 4.4 W clean into 4 ohm at 13.2 V (model), 30 dB gain, flat from 20 Hz. Volume is a dual 100k linear pot that two X9C104 digital pots (in hand) replace on the same header; mute is a toggle now and a GPIO later, wired in parallel. Every part except the pot, three film caps, the connectors and a heatsink is in the catalog.

The problem

The LA4440 has too much gain for a volume pot

The chip's gain is fixed at 51.5 dB (x376). Full power needs only 18 mV at the input, so a phone at 1 V would reach full volume in the first 2% of the pot's travel - that is tap 1 or 2 of the 100 taps on an X9C104. The volume control would be useless.

The mechanism

The datasheet lets you lower the gain with a resistor RNF' in series with the feedback capacitor on pins 1 and 7: gain = 20k / (50 + RNF'). 470 ohm gives 30 dB, so full power now needs 0.18 V and the pot uses its whole travel. "Adjustable gain" is the volume pot; RNF' sets its range. The digital pot cannot replace RNF' - its 1k steps are too coarse for a 0 to 500 ohm job.

Where the simulation corrected me

What I had wrong on paper

CORRECTED. The datasheet circuit (RNF' = 0) reaches clipping at 20 mV of input, so a 1 V source clips at 2% of pot travel. RNF' = 470 moves clipping to 0.18 V (run_gain.cir, run_fp.cir). Not optional with a 100-tap digital pot.

CORRECTED. I expected the 10k taper resistor to hide the X9C104's +/-20% tolerance. It does the opposite: the fixed 10k load makes the law depend more on the pot's own value, so 80k against 120k is 2.7 dB apart at 30 to 60% travel (run_vol.cir, with the taper R). Without it the gap is 1.4 dB. A per-channel firmware offset fixes it; a mechanical dual pot also has 1 to 3 dB gang tracking error.

CONFIRMED. Low corner 20 Hz at RNF' 470, set by 2200 uF into 4 ohm (18 Hz by hand). At RNF' 0 it is 64 Hz because the 47 uF NF cap against 50 ohm then dominates (68 Hz by hand).

CONFIRMED. Mute current (VCC - 0.7) / 1.8k = 6.9 mA, simulated 6.9 mA (run_full.cir).

LIMIT. Clean output ends at 4.4 W into 4 ohm at 13.2 V (model); the datasheet's 6 W is at 10% distortion.

MODEL. The behavioural chip (open-loop gain 3000) reads 49.4 dB at RNF' 0 against 50.0 dB expected (51.5 dB typical less 1.5 dB for R5 into the 30k input): 0.6 dB low. At 470 it agrees within 0.1 dB. The 190 kHz high corner at 30 dB is a model number, not a datasheet one.

The one to watch. Mute pop. The wiper caps C13/C14 are what the datasheet says makes AC-mute pop worse; they are there to stop pot scratch and X9C104 step clicks. If mute pops, short C13/C14 first and live with some slider noise.
Build notes

Building and adapting it

  • Verified: RNF' 1k instead of 470 gives 24.5 dB for sources hotter than 1 V (run_gain.cir).
  • Proposed: R9 1.2k raises mute current to about 10 mA if 40 dB is not reached at 6.9 mA - unsimulated attenuation, bench check.
  • Proposed: first power-up on the bench PSU at 13.2 V with a 300 mA limit and no input. Idle draw should be about 100 mA (datasheet), outputs 6.8 V.
  • Proposed: follow the datasheet layout: keep input wiring (pins 2, 6) away from the outputs (pins 10, 12), Zobels right at the pins.
  • Digital later: X9C104 powered from the ESP32's 5 V; signal stays inside its +/-5 V terminal limit. Map volume steps in firmware; tap 0 puts the wiper at ground through its 40 ohm wiper resistance, about -68 dB (hand estimate).
